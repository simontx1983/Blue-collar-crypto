Simon X has been signing blocks since genesis. No foils. No showboat delegations. Seven years of showing up. We asked him what's kept him on shift — and what he's looking for when he signs on behind a validator.

Since'19

The first time Simon delegated a stake, it was to a validator called Blacksmith Node in the winter of 2019, back when Cosmos Hub had just shipped its genesis block and nobody really knew if any of this was going to work. He put down what he could afford to lose — rent money, he'll admit now — because he thought the operator sounded like someone who'd actually show up the next morning.

Seven years later, Simon is still there. Same validator. Same stake, mostly — he's added to it twice, never withdrawn. Now he watches 142 cards across five chains, signs reviews that other delegators read before they move their money, and quietly sits on a streak of 31 consecutive days of activity on the BCC floor. He's not loud. He doesn't post foil pulls. If you search for his handle you'll find thirty-eight reviews, four disputes he's signed on to, and a single pinned quote on his profile that reads: "Never chase foils. Chase uptime."

You want to know who to trust? Watch who shows up the day after a fork. — Simon X, on what he looks for in a validator

By trade he's a machinist in Michigan — sixteen years at the same shop, union Local 342. "Crypto was never the point," he says. "The point was I wanted somewhere to put my savings that wasn't going to get eaten by the bank. And then I got curious about who was running the thing." That curiosity turned into a habit: reading validator post-mortems the way other people read box scores. He can tell you which operators disclosed their downtime and which ones didn't. He remembers which chains forked clean and which ones limped through.

Ask him what makes a good validator and he'll talk about boring things. Commission that doesn't creep up. A self-bond that stays roughly constant. A governance record where every vote has a signature behind it, even the ones nobody was watching. "You can't fake seven years of boring," he says. "That's what I'm looking at. That's the whole card."

On the BCC floor he's become something of a shop steward — not officially, just in the way other members end up tagging him when they're unsure whether a validator's explanation holds up. When Chain-X missed 4.2% of blocks in era 2847, Simon's was the second signature on the dispute. "I don't sign unless I'd say it to the operator's face," he says. "If more people signed like that, there'd be less noise, and we'd get to the real problems faster."
